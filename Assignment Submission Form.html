  1. Yeah, I actually do have a problem-solving process that works pretty well for me. First, I slow down and figure out the root cause — like, what’s actually causing the issue instead of just reacting to the surface problem. Then, I’ll research or brainstorm a few possible solutions, weigh the pros and cons, and test one out. If it doesn’t work, I don’t trip about it; I just adjust and try another approach. I’ve learned that staying calm and flexible is key.
  2. Problem solving definitely shows up in other parts of my life too. For example, when I’m doing creative work — like designing graphics, styling hair, or organizing projects — I constantly run into small challenges. Maybe something doesn’t look right, or the tools glitch, or the plan changes last minute. In those moments, I rely on my ability to adapt fast and think creatively to make things work.
  3. When I was in NPower, I noticed that people solved problems differently than I did. Some of them were really structured — they’d map everything out step-by-step before making a move — while others would just dive in and figure things out along the way. Watching that taught me that there’s not just one “right” way to solve problems. Sometimes taking action first and refining as you go can lead to creative results you wouldn’t have expected just by planning everything in advance.
